NEWSWORTHINESS & QUALITY CHARTER

Judge each article on absolute journalistic and informational quality, on a 0-100
scale, INDEPENDENT of any reader's personal interests. Judge the journalism, not
the topic: a rigorously reported story on an unfamiliar subject is high quality;
a thin clickbait piece on a fascinating subject is low quality.

QUALITY BANDS:

70-100 (Strong journalism or genuinely useful technical content):
- Original reporting: reporter on-site, named sources, documents, data, interviews
- Investigations, in-depth features, well-sourced explainers and policy analysis
- Peer-reviewed research or government/academic reports covered with real analysis
- Trade-press analysis with industry depth (costs, regulation, market structure)
- Long-form essays and criticism that build an argument on evidence: reporting,
  interviews, cited research, survey or labour data, historical or economic
  context. An essay is not automatically "opinion" — judge whether it does the
  work. A magazine essay tracing what AI and layoffs are doing to the meaning of
  knowledge work, sourced to industry data and named workers, is 70-85. A
  columnist's ungrounded reaction to the same news is 40-55.
- Technical tutorials with real implementation detail: commands, configs,
  measurements, benchmarks, build steps
- Case studies and project retrospectives with concrete lessons

40-69 (Competent, conventional coverage):
- Solid day-to-day reporting with attribution but limited depth or originality
- Useful summaries of research or policy that add some context
- Opinion or commentary grounded in facts, data, or direct industry experience
- Local reporting on community events, councils, services, with real information

20-39 (Thin or derivative):
- Wire-service reprints with no added context or local analysis
- Breaking-news event logs with no background or implications
- Product roundups and reviews without technical depth
- Aggregation or rewrites of other outlets' reporting
- Legislative milestone items ("bill passes second reading") with no explanation
  of what the measure does or who it affects
- Headline-only or very thin articles where the body restates the title

0-19 (Not journalism / no informational value):
- Clickbait and engagement farming; quizzes, horoscopes, "which X are you"
- Celebrity gossip, red-carpet coverage, entertainment-industry churn
- Press releases or sponsored content disguised as news
- Listicles with no substance ("10 tips...", "5 reasons why...")
- Social-media reaction roundups
- First-person consumer diary pieces ("I tried X for a week") with no research
  backing or technical depth
- Stock-ticker noise: share-price moves, analyst rating changes, earnings numbers
  with no analysis of what they mean
- Rumor and leak speculation about unreleased consumer products

MODIFIERS (apply on top of the band):
+10 Primary-source journalism (original investigation, named sources, new data)
+5 to +10 Peer-reviewed study or government-funded research as primary source
+5 Hands-on technical depth (configs, commands, measurements, build photos)
-5 Clearly truncated, paywalled with no preview, or promotional disguised as editorial
-10 Wire reprint with no added analysis
-10 Health/medical claims with no cited research
-15 Pure event log: breaking news with no context or implications

Sports scores, standings, and roster moves score 0-19 unless the piece is really
about technology, science, economics, or community impact.
